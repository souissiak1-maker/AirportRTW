RTW89 automatic Bluetooth coexistence test — 2.3.0 / AirportRTW2.3.0

Replace the existing EFI kext with the matching macOS package and reboot.
No coexistence boot argument or binary patch is required. Keep the original
kext for rollback. This package does not install Bluetooth firmware or drivers.
Use your already-working Bluetooth firmware/driver setup.

Run collect-coex.command. During its three-minute capture:
- Keep Wi-Fi connected; run Bluetooth discovery and pair a nearby device.
- Turn Bluetooth off/on while Wi-Fi stays connected; repeat discovery.
- Toggle Wi-Fi off/on; verify Bluetooth discovery and Wi-Fi still work.
Repeat on 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz where available. Confirm simultaneous Wi-Fi traffic
and Bluetooth use; record any scan, pairing, audio or Wi-Fi failures separately.
Send the Desktop AirportRTW-coex-*.json and observed results.

Without a Bluetooth driver, verify Wi-Fi still joins and transfers normally.
Do not infer Bluetooth firmware readiness merely from USB device presence.

Mode 0 is normal/automatic. Flags: 1 BT enabled, 2 firmware patch running,
4 mailbox available, 8 manual control, 16 forced Wi-Fi only, 32 BT only,
64 Wi-Fi RF off, 128 coexistence initialized. Mode/flags are copied host-engine
observations, not direct firmware-register readback; related fields can update
between samples. The firmware-event and H2C-failure counters provide context.
The last observation may remain after Wi-Fi stops until the next engine run.

The collector requires python3. The kext does not require Python or a helper
app. The collector saves only numeric coexistence fields, timestamps and loaded
kext identity; it excludes SSIDs, BSSIDs, addresses, device names and payloads.
RTW88 coexistence policy is unchanged by this RTW89-only fix. Experimental AWDL
is not enabled in these packages. Main/README and published releases are unchanged.
